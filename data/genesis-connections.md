# Genesis Connections — Prototype Reference

## Primary Connection: Genesis 1 ↔ John 1

**Genesis 1:1**  
“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.”

**John 1:1**  
“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”

### Evidence
- **Structural parallel**: Both open with “In the beginning” (בְּרֵאשִׁית / ἐν ἀρχῇ).
- **Creation through speech**: Genesis 1 repeatedly uses וַיֹּאֶמֶר (“and God said”). John identifies the Word (Logos) as the agent of creation (John 1:3).
- **Shared theological claim**: One God, one creative act, one ultimate reality.
- **Intertextual awareness**: Early Christian writers (e.g., John, Paul) deliberately echo Genesis 1 when speaking of Christ.

### Strength: Very High (canonical, linguistic, and theological)

## Supporting Links
- Genesis 1:3 “Let there be light” ↔ John 1:4–5, 9 (the light of the Word)
- Genesis 1:26–27 (image of God) ↔ John 1:14 (the Word became flesh)
- Broader theme nodes: Creation, Logos/Word, Light, Image

## Validation Notes
- Connection is not forced; it is one of the most widely recognized intertextual links in Scripture.
- Linguistic roots are solid (Hebrew *re’shit*, *bara*; Greek *archē*, *logos*).
- Cultural context: Ancient Near Eastern creation myths often involve speech or divine decree; the biblical text uniquely ties creation to the personal Word of the one God.
